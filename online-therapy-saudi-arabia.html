Your Working Week, and When Sessions Run

Saudi Arabia's official weekend has been Friday and Saturday since a royal order in June 2013. Government offices, banks and schools work Sunday to Thursday. Private employers must give at least Friday off; some give Saturday too.

Sessions run Monday to Saturday, so Friday and Saturday, your weekend, are both session days. Sunday, the start of your working week, is the one day without sessions.

Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam and Makkah all keep the same time: Arabia Standard Time, three hours ahead of GMT, with no daylight saving. Pakistan is two hours ahead of you all year, so the conversion never changes:

What People in Saudi Arabia Come to Therapy For

Attia's Saudi clients often arrive carrying years of unspoken weight, the kind that builds when the job is demanding, the family is far away, and everyone depends on you. Her practice is rooted in the Pakistani experience, but clients across the Kingdom also include Indian, Bangladeshi and other Muslim expats facing the same distance and the same pressures.

  • Anxiety and panic, including the worry about money and the family back home
  • Low mood and depression, which far from home often shows up as exhaustion or numbness
  • OCD, including religious OCD (waswasa)
  • Trauma and hurt from a spouse or in-laws
  • Postnatal depression, for mothers having a baby far from their own mothers
  • Marriage counseling and pre-marital counseling

If you searched for an English-speaking therapist in Riyadh or Jeddah: sessions are in English too, or in Urdu or Punjabi, and you can switch mid-sentence.

A Marriage Split Between Saudi Arabia and Pakistan

For many Pakistani families, Saudi work means years apart: a husband in Dammam, a wife and children in Pakistan, and a marriage kept going through phone calls and annual leave. Others live together in Riyadh or Jeddah, with the pressures of work here and expectations from home.

Marriage counseling with Attia is individual by default. You attend on your own, from wherever you are, and work on what is happening in the marriage; you don't need your spouse to agree before you start. If it would help, your spouse can occasionally join a session, from Saudi Arabia or from Pakistan.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the session times in Saudi Arabia?

Monday to Saturday, 8am to 6pm Saudi time (AST). That is 10am to 8pm in Pakistan, which is two hours ahead. Saudi Arabia doesn't change its clocks, so these times stay the same all year, in Riyadh, Jeddah and everywhere else in the Kingdom.

Can I book on a Friday or Saturday?

Yes. Sessions run Monday to Saturday, so both days of the Saudi weekend are session days. Sunday is the only day with no sessions.

How much does a private therapy session cost from Jeddah or Riyadh?

PKR 8,000 for a 50-minute session, roughly SAR 104 at current exchange rates. It is one flat fee for every type of session.

How do I pay from Saudi Arabia?

Ask about paying from Saudi Arabia when you message on WhatsApp; you'll get the details before you book.

I searched for a Pakistani psychologist in Riyadh. Is this the same thing?

Not quite. Attia Altaf is a UK-qualified (CPCAB) Integrative Psychotherapist, not a psychologist or a psychiatrist, and she doesn't prescribe medication. For talk therapy, such as anxiety, depression, trauma or marriage problems, a qualified psychotherapist is the right person to see.

My wife and children are in Pakistan and I work in Saudi Arabia. Can we still get marriage counseling?

Yes. Counseling is individual by default, so you can start on your own from Saudi Arabia, and your wife can start from Pakistan. If it would help, a spouse can occasionally join a session, even from another country.

In an emergency: if you are thinking of harming yourself or are in danger, don't wait for a session. In Saudi Arabia, call 997 for an ambulance (the Saudi Red Crescent's national emergency number) or go to the nearest hospital emergency department.
