Frequently Asked Questions

Can I see a Pakistani therapist online if I live in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. Healing with Attia offers online therapy via Zoom to Pakistani, South Asian, and Muslim clients across Saudi Arabia — Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, Makkah, Madinah and beyond. Sessions are in Urdu, English, or Punjabi.

What are the session timings for Saudi clients?

Sessions run Mon–Sat, 10am–8pm Pakistan time — that is 8am–6pm Saudi time (AST), just two hours behind. After-work and weekend slots are easy to arrange, usually within the same week.

How much does online therapy cost from Saudi Arabia?

PKR 5,000 per 50-minute session — roughly SAR 65 at current exchange rates. Private therapy in Riyadh or Jeddah typically costs several hundred riyals per session. Same professional standard, a fraction of the price.

Can I have therapy sessions in Urdu from Saudi Arabia?

Yes — Urdu, English, or Punjabi, switching freely mid-session. For many clients in Saudi Arabia, Urdu is the language their feelings actually live in.

Is therapy allowed and confidential in Saudi Arabia?

Seeking therapy is a private healthcare choice, and your sessions are exactly that — private. Everything happens online via secure Zoom from your own space. Nothing is shared with employers, sponsors, family, or anyone else.

I searched for a Pakistani psychologist in Riyadh — is this the same thing?

Attia Altaf is a UK-certified Integrative Psychotherapist (CPCAB), not a psychologist — but for talk therapy (anxiety, depression, trauma, marriage issues) a qualified psychotherapist is exactly the right professional. Read: psychiatrist vs psychologist vs psychotherapist.

Do you offer marriage counselling for couples in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. Both spouses can join the same Zoom session — from the same home in Riyadh, or one in Saudi Arabia and one in Pakistan, which is very common when work separates families. Pre-marital counselling before nikah is also available.

Is faith respected in therapy?

Deeply. Attia works with — never against — your faith. Therapy does not replace deen; it clears the anxiety, depression, or trauma that gets in the way of living it with presence. Religious OCD (waswasa) is a particular specialty.
