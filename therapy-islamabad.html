Marriage Counseling in Islamabad and Rawalpindi

One-to-one, on Zoom, with a therapist who lives in Islamabad.

Marriage counseling with Attia is always one-to-one. You talk with her on your own, about your side of the marriage, and your spouse is never in the session. You can begin whether or not they believe in therapy.

Here, a marriage can look fine from the outside for years, and the first hurdle is usually saying out loud that it isn't. You can do that in Urdu, English or Punjabi, from home or from a private room at work.

What people in Islamabad bring to marriage counseling

  • Joint family pressure & in-law dynamics

Does my spouse have to take part?

No. Your spouse never joins and never needs to know. The sessions are about what you can change: how you respond in an argument, which boundaries you hold with family, and what you want from the marriage.

Good to Know Before Your First Session

If the call drops

Attia is in Islamabad too, so a dropped call can come from either end: a power cut, a router restarting, a weak signal. Reconnect, and the two of you carry on.

When online therapy isn't the right fit

If you are at risk of harming yourself or another person, if you may need medication, or if you are not safe where you live, start with a hospital or the emergency services rather than online sessions. Attia doesn't prescribe; psychiatrists, who are medical doctors, do. In Islamabad, PIMS in G-8 has a psychiatry department, and the police are on 15.

For most people the evidence backs online work: UCLA Health points to more than 60 studies in which seeing a therapist by video was just as effective as being there in person for anxiety, depression and PTSD, though it says severe conditions call for face-to-face care.
