Marriage Counseling in Karachi

If you have been looking for a marriage counselor in Karachi, these are the questions people usually ask before they book. How Attia works with marriages is explained in full in the marriage counseling guide.

Can I start without my spouse?

Yes. It is the only way Attia works: marriage counseling with her is always one-to-one. You work on your side of the marriage (how you speak, where you hold a line, what you do when an argument starts) and your spouse never joins a session.

Will my family or my in-laws find out?

Not from Attia. Nothing is shared with your family, your in-laws or anyone else, and sessions are never recorded. If you live with family, the practical problem is usually a private place to talk: some clients take the call in a parked car, others wait for an hour when the house is out. Headphones help.

Do you offer pre-marital sessions?

Yes, one-to-one, before the nikah: what you expect from marriage, money, where you will live, and how to bring up the hard topics with your future spouse. There is more in the pre-marital counseling guide.

What happens in the first session?

You tell Attia what is happening in your marriage and what you want to change. She asks about the history, the families involved and what you have already tried, and by the end you agree what the next sessions will focus on. You don't have to decide anything about the marriage in that first hour.

What does it cost?

The same flat fee as any other session, with nothing extra for marriage work. It is in the fees section below, next to what Karachi practices charge.

Choosing a Therapist in Karachi: Clinic or Online

Plenty of people searching here are weighing up Karachi practices they have heard of by name. Whoever you are considering, Attia included, ask them five things before the first session:

  1. What is your training, and who awarded it? A named awarding body is something you can check.
  2. Which languages can we use? What hurts most often comes out in Urdu or Punjabi, even for people who work in English.
  3. What is the fee, and what happens if I cancel?
  4. Can you prescribe medication? Only a psychiatrist, who is a medical doctor, can. Attia does not prescribe. See psychiatrist, psychologist or psychotherapist.
  5. Are sessions recorded? Attia's never are.

When a clinic is the better choice

If there is nowhere at home you can talk without being overheard, or you simply think better sitting across from someone, an in-person clinic may suit you more than Zoom. That is a fair reason, not a failure. Just count the drive across Karachi at rush hour when you compare fees. There is more in how to choose a therapist in Pakistan.

Two Things to Know Before You Book

If the call drops

A K-Electric outage or a weak connection can cut a call, and Attia is in Pakistan too, so it can happen on either side. If it does, you reconnect and carry on.

When online therapy isn't the right first step

Online one-to-one therapy is not where to start if you are at risk of harming yourself or someone else, if you may need medication, or if you are not safe at home. Attia does not prescribe; a psychiatrist, who is a medical doctor, does. In Karachi, the psychiatry departments at JPMC and Civil Hospital Karachi, Karwan-e-Hayat's psychiatric services, and Rescue 1122 in an emergency are the places to go (numbers in the box above).

For most other people, online works: a UCLA Health summary of more than 60 studies found it as effective as face-to-face sessions for anxiety, depression and PTSD, while recommending in-person care for more severe conditions.
